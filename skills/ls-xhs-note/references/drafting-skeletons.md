# Drafting Skeletons

Use this file in Step 4 after the framework is chosen.

The goal is to keep the draft native to the platform:

- fast first screen
- short blocks
- clear save point
- easy downstream visual slicing
- visible posting rhythm instead of article rhythm

## Shared output contract

Every draft should contain:

- one title line at the top
- one opening hook area within the first `2-4` lines
- `4-8` short body paragraphs
- one closing interaction line
- one hashtag block at the bottom

Default length target:

- compact note: `250-450` Chinese characters
- standard note: `450-800` Chinese characters
- dense note: `800-1100` Chinese characters

Formatting rule:

- final copy is saved in `.md`, but it should not read like article Markdown
- avoid `#`, `##`, and section-heading dependency in the final note
- use line breaks to create rhythm
- use complete sentences or short natural paragraphs, not stacked noun phrases
- use `2-5` emoji anchors across title, body, and ending when they improve scanning
- body emojis should mark a shift, a warning, a checklist item, or a conclusion
- do not add emoji to every line

First-screen rule:

- line 1 should carry the tension or result
- line 2 should sharpen the mistake, hidden cost, or hidden rule
- by line 4, the reader should already know why the note is worth saving

Interaction rule:

- use one natural question at the end
- ask for a bottleneck, a choice, or the next part to expand
- do not ask for likes, follows, or saves directly
- one closing emoji is allowed if it helps the ending feel lighter and more native

## 1. Pain-Point

```md
{title line}

{hook sentence or short paragraph: visible pain, hidden cost, or repeating mistake}
{hook sentence or short paragraph: wrong assumption or why the usual fix keeps failing}

{body paragraph: what people keep treating as the problem}
{body paragraph: what the real blockage is}
{body paragraph: the first useful fix}
{body paragraph: what stops repeating after the fix}

{closing interaction line}
{hashtags}
```

Drafting notes:

- the first useful fix should appear early
- every block should answer one question only
- short contrast sentences work well here
- if the pain is weak, do not force this framework

## 2. Story

```md
{title line}

{hook sentence or short paragraph: the turning point or reversal}
{hook sentence or short paragraph: why that moment mattered}

{body paragraph: minimum context}
{body paragraph: what was assumed before}
{body paragraph: what changed}
{body paragraph: what rule can be reused now}

{closing interaction line}
{hashtags}
```

Drafting notes:

- background should be compressed hard
- one decision matters more than full chronology
- one scene is enough if the lesson lands fast
- the ending should point to reuse, not emotion alone

## 3. Listicle

```md
{title line}

{hook sentence or short paragraph: why the bundle is worth saving}
{hook sentence or short paragraph: what most people miss before they open the list}

{body paragraph: point one + why it matters}
{body paragraph: point two + why it matters}
{body paragraph: point three + why it matters}
{optional body paragraph: point four if the value rises}
{compression paragraph: one rule that ties the list together}

{closing interaction line}
{hashtags}
```

Drafting notes:

- every point must carry a consequence
- avoid equal-length filler blocks
- let the value rise instead of staying flat
- the list should feel cumulative

## 4. Comparison

```md
{title line}

{hook sentence or short paragraph: the visible decision}
{hook sentence or short paragraph: what the comparison should really be based on}

{body paragraph: why the common comparison standard is weak}
{body paragraph: option A through consequence}
{body paragraph: option B through consequence}
{body paragraph: who should choose which and why}

{closing interaction line}
{hashtags}
```

Drafting notes:

- compare through outcomes, not generic pros and cons
- lead with the hidden decision rule early
- give a reader-fit answer near the end
- if one option is clearly weak, change frameworks

## 5. Judgment Breakdown

```md
{title line}

{hook sentence or short paragraph: the visible signal}
{hook sentence or short paragraph: why the obvious reading misses the useful layer}

{body paragraph: what people are concluding too fast}
{body paragraph: the deeper judgment}
{body paragraph: the mechanism behind it}
{body paragraph: what changes if that judgment is right}

{closing interaction line}
{hashtags}
```

Drafting notes:

- the hidden layer must arrive early
- each block should push the judgment forward
- contradiction works better than a neutral summary
- do not drift into broad commentary

## 6. Technical Breakdown

```md
{title line}

{hook sentence or short paragraph: what keeps feeling messy or failing}
{hook sentence or short paragraph: the one model that simplifies it}

{body paragraph: the model in plain words}
{body paragraph: key move or step}
{body paragraph: key move or step}
{body paragraph: common failure mode or execution check}

{closing interaction line}
{hashtags}
```

Drafting notes:

- use plain terms first, formal terms second
- make the body workable even before images are added
- numbered or emoji-led micro-steps are allowed when they speed up scanning
- keep the model tighter than the article version would be
